# Lenses

The question bank for `product-thinking`, one section per lens, in interview order. Pick the questions the idea needs; tags like [S3] point to [Sources](#sources).

## 1. User and job

- Who exactly has this problem? Name a real person or segment, not "users".
- In what circumstance does it come up, and what progress do they want to make? [S5]
- What do they use today instead, including doing nothing or a workaround? [S5]
- Push and pull: what about today frustrates them, and what draws them to something new? Anxiety and habit: what fear or routine keeps them where they are? [S6]
- Is this a few people who want it badly, or many who want it a little? Prefer the first. [S1]
- Would we use it ourselves?

## 2. Problem evidence

- When did this last happen to someone you can name? What did they do about it? [S4]
- What did it cost them in money, time or risk? Did they search for, pay for or build a fix?
- Which part of this is something you saw, and which part is your guess? [S4]
- Example push-back: "users want a family plan" → who asked last month, and what did they do instead: share one account, buy two, or leave?
- What is the insight: what do we know or can we do now that others cannot, and why now? [S11]

## 3. Four risks

- **Value**: will they choose it or pay for it over what they use today? [S3]
- **Usability**: can they figure it out without help? [S3]
- **Feasibility**: can we build it with the time, skills and technology we have? [S3]
- **Viability**: does it work for the business (cost, support, legal, channels, brand)? [S3]
- Does it delight customers in a way that is hard to copy and improves margin? [S10]
- Which of these risks is the largest? That one goes in the verdict.

## 4. Customer narrative

- Write the one-paragraph launch announcement from the customer's view. Is the headline something they would care about? [S9]
- Which question in its FAQ is the hardest to answer honestly? [S9]

## 5. Smallest test

- What is the riskiest assumption, stated so a test can prove it false? [S2]
- What is the cheapest test of that assumption: a conversation, a manual or fake version, a landing page, a prototype? [S2]
- Can we deliver it by hand to the first users before building anything? [S7]

## 6. Metric and kill criterion

- Which number tells us the test worked, and what threshold counts as go?
- What result makes us stop, decided now, before we see the data?
- For an existing product: what share of users would be very disappointed without it? 40% is the reference bar. [S8]

## 7. Pre-mortem

- It is three months after launch and it failed spectacularly. Why? [S12, S13]
- **Tigers**: clear threats that hurt us unless we act. **Paper tigers**: threats you do not worry about but others might. **Elephants**: what nobody is saying. [S13]
- Which tiger does the cheapest test or the plan not cover yet?
